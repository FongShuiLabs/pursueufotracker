What does PURSUE stand for?

Presidential Unsealings and Reporting System for UAP Encounters. It is the Trump administration's official program to declassify and publicly release U.S. government records on Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena (UAP), spanning the FBI, the Department of War, NASA, and the State Department. The program landing page is at war.gov/UFO.

For a comprehensive explainer on how the rolling-release program is structured, the agency breakdown (Release 01 = 82 DoD + 57 FBI + 15 NASA + 7 State = 161; Release 02 added 52 DoD + 7 NASA + 5 CIA/ODNI/DOE = 64 for 222 combined; Release 03 added 12 DoD + 29 FBI + 11 NASA + 18 CIA + 2 IC/government = 72 for 294 combined), the May 11 CSV revision, and the boundaries of what PURSUE actually establishes, see our PURSUE program deep dive.

Do these files prove aliens exist?

No. None of the released files contains a body, a craft, biological material, or any artifact whose origin can only be extraterrestrial. They also do not prove aliens don't exist.

What they DO show is that trained military and government personnel have reported observations the federal record continues to log as "unresolved," and that the U.S. government has been collecting such reports continuously for at least 80 years. Among the highest-scoring files in the archive - eight are tied at the top score of 72 - is the December 1965 Gemini 7 air-to-ground audio in which astronaut Frank Borman reports an unidentified object to NASA mission control - see our Borman Incident deep dive. The densest scoring band is the 96 files tied at 66 - unresolved military mission captures, anchored by the 27 submitted to AARO - see our AARO Unresolved UAP deep dive.

What's the difference between UFO and UAP?

UFO (Unidentified Flying Object) is the historical, public-facing term used since the 1940s. UAP (Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena, formerly Unidentified Aerial Phenomena until 2022) is the current official U.S. government term, adopted to cover underwater and transmedium objects, not just aerial ones.

The Pentagon, AARO, and ODNI use UAP in formal documents. War.gov runs the PURSUE program but kept the public URL at /UFO/ because that's the term most people search.

What is the Anomalousness Index?

A 0-100 score reflecting evidentiary weight that an encounter remains unexplained after conventional analysis. It is NOT a probability of extraterrestrial origin. Anyone publishing a "percent chance aliens" number is selling something.

The score is calculated from six weighted components:

  • Sensor quality (weight 0.25)
  • Witness credibility (0.20)
  • Corroboration (0.20)
  • Kinematic anomaly (0.15)
  • Mundane-explanation availability (0.10)
  • Official disposition (0.10)

The full rubric and weights are open JSON at /data/scoring-rubric.json. Anyone can recompute every score on this site. The rubric in practice produces eight files tied at the top score of 72 (the Gemini 7 Borman audio, the Gordon Cooper interview, and the Apollo 14, 16, and 17 debriefings), 96 files tied at 66 (the AARO-submitted and Release-02 military captures), 9 files at 65 (the Apollo 12 lunar photographs and the Apollo 17 triangular-formation image), and a long tail of scoring tiers below.

How can I verify a file matches what's on war.gov?

Every file has a SHA-256 hash on its detail page and in the public verification manifest. Steps:

  1. Re-download the file from war.gov/UFO
  2. Hash it locally:
    • Windows PowerShell: Get-FileHash file.pdf -Algorithm SHA256
    • macOS / Linux: shasum -a 256 file.pdf
  3. Compare against the SHA-256 on our detail page. They should match exactly.

This is how journalists fact-check our archive.

Where do the videos come from?

The video files in PURSUE are hosted on DVIDS (Defense Visual Information Distribution Service), the U.S. military's public media platform. We retrieve them via DVIDS' public API.

Each video page includes the closed captions provided by DVIDS and a Whisper-generated full transcript, and we link back to the official DVIDS page on every video detail page.

Is the viral "two-tiered structure" Atlantic UFO video real?

The video is real: DOW-UAP-PR116, a 32-second infrared sensor capture from a 2020 Atlantic Ocean military encounter, released in PURSUE Drop 04 (July 10, 2026) and submitted by U.S. Northern Command to AARO. The "two-tiered structure" description is a social-media characterization - that phrase appears nowhere in the released record.

What the record does contain: a paired Range Fouler debrief (DOW-UAP-D091) in which the military observer describes the object as "darker, maroonish color, approximately 12-15 feet in height," notes it "travel[ing] with the wind" without maneuvering, and says it appeared "similar to a 'large, somewhat deformed balloon.'" War.gov explicitly cautions that the observer's language is subjective interpretation, not a determination - the file remains formally unresolved. Both documents, read quote by quote: DOW-UAP-PR116, Explained.

Why do the archive's highest-scoring files describe an explained phenomenon?

Because the Anomalousness Index measures evidence quality, not mystery. Four files tied at the archive's top score of 72 are Apollo 14 and Apollo 17 crew debriefings about the "light flash phenomena" - streaks of light astronauts perceived, often with their eyes closed, now understood as high-energy cosmic-ray particles striking the retina.

They score at the top because astronaut testimony on the official federal record is the rubric's highest witness tier, the recordings are preserved official debriefings, and they were released as open records. None of the six axes asks "is the cause unknown?" A high score means the evidence chain is strong - not that the object is unidentified. Full walkthrough, with the crews' verbatim words: The Astronaut Light Flashes.

Do you use AI? How?

Yes, for three things, all human-supervised:

  • Rubric application. Claude (Anthropic) reads each file's publicly reported description and selects which rubric value matches each of the six components. The rubric and weights are human-designed and live as open JSON.
  • Video transcription. OpenAI Whisper generates the .vtt subtitle tracks and full transcripts you see on every video that has spoken audio (most PURSUE videos are silent sensor captures).
  • PDF text extraction. pdfplumber (open-source, not AI) pulls searchable text out of every PDF.

We do not use AI to decide whether files prove aliens exist. No model can honestly do that and we refuse to publish such a number.
